Getting started

The first time you open VehicleVault, it asks to show notifications. Tap Turn on reminders and allow it — reminders are the whole point of the app. If you chose Not now, the app asks again when you save your first document, or you can turn notifications on later from Settings.

There's no account to create. Everything you add is encrypted and stays on your phone.

Add a vehicle

  1. Open the Vehicles tab and tap Add vehicle.
  2. Type the Registration number. Spaces and dashes don't matter — INDMH 12 AB 1234 and mh12ab1234 are the same vehicle.
  3. Pick the type — car, two-wheeler, commercial or other. Make, model, year, colour and fuel are optional.
  4. Tap Save.

The app shows each vehicle by its number plate: white for private vehicles, yellow for commercial, green for electric.

Add a document

  1. Open a vehicle and tap Add document.
  2. Choose what it is: Registration (RC), Insurance, PUC, FASTag or Other.
  3. Add a photo with Take photo or Choose from gallery. This is optional, but handy when someone asks to see the paper.
  4. Set the Expiry date. This is what reminders are based on.
  5. Optionally add the Issue date, Issuer (for example your insurance company) and notes.
  6. Tap Save. Reminders are set up straight away.
Tip: add the issue date too. With it, the vehicle page shows a bar of how much of the document's validity is left.

Renewed a document? Open it, tap the edit (pencil) button, set the new expiry date and use Retake for the new photo. The reminders move by themselves.

Reading the Home screen

  • The gauge counts down the days to your next renewal. It's full from 30 days out and empties as the date gets closer. Tap it to open that document.
  • Road-ready shows how many of your vehicles have no expired documents. Each vehicle has a light: mint means fine, red means something has expired, grey means it has no documents yet.
  • Expired, Due soon and Valid count all your documents. A document is due soon from 30 days before its expiry date. It's still valid on the expiry date itself, and expired from the next day.
  • Needs attention lists everything expired or due soon, soonest first.

The lock chip at the top is a reminder that everything you see is encrypted on this phone.

Reminders

For each document, VehicleVault reminds you 30, 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before it expires, and on the day. Tap a reminder to open that document.

  • Change the time: Settings → Reminder time. The default is 9:00 AM.
  • Check that they work: Settings → Send a test notification.
  • See what's scheduled: Settings shows how many reminders are set. If it says reminders could not be scheduled, follow the steps in the next section.

Reminders are scheduled on your phone when you save a document. The app doesn't need to be open, and there's no server involved. A reminder can arrive a few minutes after the set time — Android groups alarms to save battery.

Reminders not arriving?

Some phones — especially Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO, Realme, Oppo, Vivo and some OnePlus models — aggressively stop apps to save battery, and that can block reminders. First make sure notifications are on (Settings → Apps → VehicleVault → Notifications), then allow the app to run in the background:

PhoneWhat to change
Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO (MIUI / HyperOS)Settings → Apps → Manage apps → VehicleVault: turn on Autostart, and set Battery saver to No restrictions.
Realme, Oppo (ColorOS / Realme UI)Settings → Apps → App management → VehicleVault → Battery usage: allow background activity and auto launch.
Vivo (Funtouch / OriginOS)Settings → Battery → Background power consumption: allow VehicleVault. Also turn on Autostart in i Manager → App manager.
Samsung (One UI)Settings → Apps → VehicleVault → Battery → Unrestricted. Make sure it isn't in the "Sleeping apps" list.
OnePlusSettings → Apps → VehicleVault → Battery usage → Allow background activity.
Other Android phonesSettings → Apps → VehicleVault → Battery → Unrestricted or Don't optimise.

Menu names differ a little between phone models and Android versions. After changing them, use Send a test notification to check.

Backup and restore

Your documents live only on your phone. If the phone is lost or reset, they're gone — unless you have a backup. The app reminds you on the Home screen if you haven't backed up in a while.

Your first backup

  1. Go to Settings → Backup and tap Back up now.
  2. The app shows a recovery code of 24 letters and numbers. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe, away from your phone.
  3. Tap I've written it down, type the code back to confirm, and tap Confirm and back up.
  4. Choose where to save the backup file — Google Drive, email to yourself, or a computer.

Later backups use the same code, so you only do this once. Lost the paper? Settings → Backup → Show recovery code shows it again while you still have this phone.

Keep the code safe. The backup is encrypted with it. Without the code, nobody can open the backup — not you, and not us. We can't recover it for you.

Restoring a backup

  1. Go to Settings → Backup → Restore from a backup and pick the backup file.
  2. Enter the recovery code.
  3. Confirm with Replace everything.

A restore replaces everything in the app — all vehicles, documents and the trash — with what's in the backup. Your reminders are set up again automatically.

Moving to a new phone

  1. On the old phone: Settings → Backup → Back up now, and save the file somewhere the new phone can reach (Drive or email is easiest).
  2. On the new phone: install VehicleVault, then Settings → Backup → Restore from a backup.
  3. Pick the file, enter your recovery code, and confirm.

Android's own phone-to-phone transfer won't move VehicleVault's data. That's deliberate: the encryption keys never leave your old phone, so only a VehicleVault backup can carry your documents across.

Deleting and the trash

  • Deleted vehicles and documents go to the trash first. Restore them from Settings → Trash → Restore.
  • Items in the trash are removed for good after 30 days. Each item shows the date.
  • Deleting a vehicle deletes its documents too. Restoring the vehicle brings them back with it.
  • Right after deleting, you can also tap Undo at the bottom of the screen.

Privacy settings

Under Settings → Privacy you choose what, if anything, leaves your phone:

  • Send crash reports (on by default): technical details when the app crashes, so we can fix it. Never your documents, vehicle numbers, dates or photos.
  • Share usage statistics (off by default): anonymous counts, such as how many documents are added. Never anything personal.

Questions

Does VehicleVault need the internet?

No. Everything works offline. The only things that ever use the internet are crash reports and usage statistics if you allow them, and backups you choose to save to Drive or email.

Can you see my documents?

No. There's no server that holds them. They're encrypted on your phone with a key that never leaves it.

Is it free?

Yes. Encryption and reminders are free for everyone, always.

Do I need an account?

No. There's no sign-up, no login and no password.

I lost my phone. Can I get my documents back?

Only from a backup. Install VehicleVault on your new phone and restore your latest backup file with your recovery code. Without a backup, the documents can't be recovered — they were never stored anywhere else.

I forgot my recovery code.

If you still have the phone you backed up from, Settings → Backup → Show recovery code shows it. If that phone is gone, the backup can't be opened — make a fresh backup as soon as you can, and keep the new code safe.

I have a lot of documents. Will every reminder still come?

Yes. The app keeps the nearest 50 reminders scheduled with the phone at any time, and tops them up whenever you open the app or change a document.

Is there an iPhone version?

Not yet. VehicleVault is on Android first; an iPhone version is planned.

Still stuck?

Write to support@appcraft.solutions with your phone model and what you're seeing. Please don't send photos of your documents — we never need them.
